# Bachelor_Thesis

## Paying for Performance: Are Swedish Active Funds Worth the Extra Cost?

Bachelor's thesis by Fabian Lindh and William Jarkander, Department of Statistics, Uppsala University (Spring 2025). Supervisor: Lars Forsberg.

Folder: `Paying_for_Performance_Swedish_Active_Funds/` (thesis PDF, R code and SQL queries)

### Abstract

This study investigates whether Swedish active funds provide value to investors relative to their fund fees. Using Swedish fund data with 12 passive and 25 active equity funds from 2015 to 2025, the performance measure alpha was extracted using a Jensen single-factor and a Fama-French three-factor model. By applying a bootstrap technique, the funds with the highest alphas were then evaluated to see if active fund managers picked stocks well enough to more than cover their fund fees while controlling for luck. Two-sample t-tests were also performed on total return and risk-adjusted performance (Sharpe ratio) to analyze differences.

The results show that actively managed funds do not on average, outperform passive funds. Passive funds delivered more stable and consistent results while active funds exhibited a larger variation in return, Sharpe ratio, and alpha. Only one fund had a significant alpha from the bootstrap analysis, indicating that these outcomes are rare. This specific result was based on limited data (2015 to 2019), affecting its reliability. Overall, the results showed that while active funds do manage to generate higher returns, fees erode these returns, making passive funds the better option on average. In conclusion, there is no evidence supporting that active funds are a better choice, especially since only a minority of active funds manage to outperform after fees, while controlling for luck.
